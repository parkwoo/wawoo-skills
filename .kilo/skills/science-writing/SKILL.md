# Science Writing (popular science)

Write about science for a **non-specialist** audience without losing accuracy.

## Workflow (pairs with `/writing`)

1. **Audience & goal**: who reads this, what they already know, the one takeaway.
2. **Ground**: use `research` on primary sources; keep citations. Mark inference vs evidence.
3. **Explain**: one idea per paragraph; define terms on first use; use a concrete analogy; avoid hype.
4. **Check**: numbers/units, uncertainty, and "correlation ≠ causation".
5. **Draft**: `writing-fragments` → `writing-beats` → `writing-shape`.
6. **Review**: fact-check with `research`; run the checklist below.
7. **Layout** (optional): OpenDesign `article-magazine` / `blog-post` (else Markdown).

## Accuracy rules

- Cite sources; never invent studies, quotes, or numbers.
- State uncertainty and effect size; distinguish mechanism from correlation.
- Note known conflicts of interest / industry funding.
- Do not overstate ("cures", "proves"); prefer "associated with", "suggests".

## Anti-hype checklist

- [ ] Headline claim is supported by the body and the source
- [ ] Units and magnitudes are correct
- [ ] Sample / limitations are mentioned
- [ ] No false balance or single-study hype
- [ ] Plain-language terms are defined

## Output

- Article Markdown + a source list + an "unverified" note for anything not confirmed.
